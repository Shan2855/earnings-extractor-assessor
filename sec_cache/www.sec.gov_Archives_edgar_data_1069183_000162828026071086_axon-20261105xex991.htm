Axon reports record Q3 2026 revenue of $909 million, up 28% year over year

•Announces increased stake in Skydio to 37%, cementing leadership in Drone as First Responder
•Software & Services revenue growth accelerates to 43% year over year, reaches $437 million
•Platform Solutions revenue grows 106% year over year to $125 million
•AI bookings up over 50% year to date; AI ARR surpasses $100 million
•Net income of $57 million increases from a net loss in the prior-year period; Adjusted EBITDA of $236 million increases 33% year over year
Fellow shareholders,

Today, we announce the largest single investment in Axon’s history: a $1.2 billion investment in Skydio. Skydio is one of America’s leading drone technology companies, and this investment increases our ownership stake to 37%. Axon has also entered into an exclusive public safety distribution partnership with Skydio in the U.S. and Canada, further solidifying our position in one of the most transformative technologies of the coming decade. Drones are already helping agencies cut response times dramatically. We expect millions of flying robots to reshape American life in the years ahead and we intend to be at the center of it.

Axon remains on pace to deliver another record year, expecting full-year revenue growth of approximately 30% on top of four consecutive years of growth above 30%. Demand is broad, Software & Services is accelerating and newer businesses are becoming meaningful contributors to our results. The reduction from our prior expectations is concentrated in three large Federal Dedrone deployments representing approximately $130 million of revenue we no longer expect in 2026. Had those deployments remained on pace, our full-year growth outlook would be nearly 500 basis points higher than our current guidance and approximately 200 basis points above our prior guidance — underscoring the robust demand we see across our business.

Software & Services revenue accelerated to 43% year over year growth and reached $437 million. Growth is increasingly driven by our newer software offerings: real-time operations, Axon Records, Axon AI, Axon 911 and Dedrone software collectively reached an annual revenue run rate of nearly $500 million, more than doubling from last year. AI annual recurring revenue surpassed $100 million.

Connected Devices revenue increased 16% year over year to $472 million. Platform Solutions led Connected Devices growth, increasing 106% to $125 million, and underlying performance would have been even stronger absent the delayed timing of an approximately $30 million Federal Dedrone deployment we had expected to recognize during the quarter.

Demand keeps building, and customers are putting more Axon to work in more markets. New five-year bookings are up 25% year to date. Select third-quarter wins show the size and breadth of what customers are deploying:
•$258 million AI Era Plan and body camera expansion with a state Department of Corrections
•$115 million OSP 10 Premium, AI Era Plan and Axon Fusus agreement with a state Department of Corrections
•$40 million OSP 10 Premium and Axon Fleet 3 Advanced expansion with a major metro police department
•$21 million TASER 10 agreement with an international police agency
•$18 million Dedrone deployment with a U.S. federal agency
•$10 million TASER and body-worn camera agreement with an enterprise healthcare provider



We are meeting that demand with an accelerating pace of innovation. In October we introduced Axon Body 5, our latest-generation body camera, and TASER Ion, a new, more flexible TASER device built for a broader range of users and use cases. We launched Axon Guardian, the latest addition to our suite of superintelligence products, and took important early steps toward Axon Gravity, our broader vision to further bring superintelligence capabilities across the public safety workflow. Together, these innovations expand the problems we can solve for customers across more verticals and geographies and open new avenues for durable, long-term growth.

We enter the fourth quarter with high conviction. We expect fourth-quarter revenue in a range of $990 million to $1.01 billion and Adjusted EBITDA of $245 million to $250 million. Our updated full-year outlook excludes approximately $130 million of revenue from the Federal Dedrone deployments discussed above, including the $30 million deployment previously expected in the third quarter and two additional deployments totaling $100 million previously expected in the fourth quarter.

Axon’s long-term position is stronger than ever. We will finish 2026 with a broader portfolio, deeper customer relationships and more avenues for growth than at any point in our history.

Product & Ecosystem
Axon is building a platform, not a product line. Our latest launches bring more intelligence to the edge, extend our less-lethal portfolio and connect more of the response lifecycle through a common ecosystem.

Skydio
Drones are becoming one of the defining technologies of the decade, and we are taking a major stake in a market leader. Today, we announce an additional $1.2 billion investment in Skydio, which takes our fully diluted ownership to 37%. Axon is also now the exclusive distributor of Skydio for public safety in the U.S. and Canada. Deployed Skydio docks through Axon Air have more than doubled year over year, and we believe Drone as First Responder (DFR) is becoming the new standard of response.

Key highlights include:
•A major stake in a category-defining company: We believe Skydio is positioned to lead in nearly every sector that flying robots touch. We now own 37% of this category definer.
•Exclusive reach in public safety: Axon becomes Skydio’s exclusive public safety distributor in the U.S. and Canada. This extends our lead in public safety technology.
•Scale that already exists: Skydio expects about $1 billion of bookings this fiscal year. It is already operating at scale.
•A faster, more complete response: Combined with Axon 911, Axon Fusus, digital evidence and Dedrone airspace security, Skydio completes an integrated platform that helps agencies detect, understand and respond faster.
•Pure-margin revenue to Axon: Axon acts as a direct channel partner. Revenue is recognized on a net basis in Software & Services.

Two companies, each a leader in its field, are building something bigger than either could alone. Axon extends its network of mission-critical technologies in public safety. Skydio stays free to move fast and build the future of American aerial robotics across many industries.

Axon Body 5
In October we introduced Axon Body 5, a major leap for the camera at the center of our ecosystem. It is built as an increasingly intelligent endpoint across the Axon platform.
Enhancements include:
•25x more processing power vs. prior-generation Axon cameras, creating a stronger hardware foundation to support current and future superintelligence capabilities directly on the device



•Improved connectivity to support public safety 5G network utilization and enable future network resilience with the ability to move between providers and adjust bandwidth in weaker connectivity settings for better live-streaming continuity
•20% longer battery life to provide full shift battery assurance and enhance reliability and performance as camera workloads continue to expand and new superintelligence innovations become available
•4K video and optional Pro model with a dedicated near-focus camera for scanning driver’s licenses and connecting relevant information into Axon Fusus
•Improved audio and optional Pro model front-facing speakers to support capabilities such as real-time translation through Axon Assistant

This is more than a hardware upgrade. Beyond a better camera, Axon Body 5 puts more intelligence directly in the field as our portfolio of superintelligence products expands.

AI Era Plan
Alongside the launch of Axon Body 5, we expanded features available in our suite of superintelligence products, the AI Era Plan, by moving Axon Guardian into general availability. Axon Guardian introduces a new category of field intelligence designed to use connected devices to enhance safety as events unfold. Its first capabilities include:
•Escalation Detection: When an Axon Body 4 or Body 5 camera is recording, Guardian can detect signals of potential conflict in real time and create an alert in Axon Fusus. A remote user can then review the livestream and determine whether additional support is needed.
•Approach Alert: On Axon Fleet 3, Guardian can detect when the door of a stopped vehicle opens and alert the officer through both the in-car display and their body-worn camera.

These features mark another step in the evolution of delivering superintelligence capabilities for our customers and showcase where we believe the future is going – connected, intelligent devices at the edge with superintelligence-powered software that supports customers before, during and after the moment.

“When Tampa became the first major agency to adopt Axon’s AI Era Plan, we recognized the potential for AI to do much more than simply automate tasks. It could give officers valuable time back, improve communication, and help them make better use of information, ultimately allowing them to focus more of their time and energy on fighting crime and serving the community.

Now, as I help educate the next generation of public safety leaders, I am even more convinced that we are at the beginning of a fundamental transformation in how technology can support our profession. What makes Axon’s approach special is that its AI technology is being designed around the real-world responsibilities, challenges, and mission of public safety.” — Lee Bercaw, Retired Tampa Police Chief and Associate Professor of Instruction, University of South Florida

TASER Ion
Intelligence helps customers understand a situation. When intervention is required, less-lethal technology is still essential. TASER Ion is an entirely new device designed to extend the reach of our TASER products. This pocket-sized form factor expands the application of the technology into environments where our traditional devices can be less practical.

Examples include undercover operations, personal and executive protection and use cases where attire, visibility or limited carry space may otherwise leave personnel with fewer options between physical engagement and a firearm. We believe this highly capable device creates an opportunity to expand our existing TASER user base, and we have already seen demand beginning to build just weeks after its introduction.

Key capabilities include:
•Flexible, discreet carry: A palm-sized, non-pistol design with belt, inside-the-waistband, deep-pocket and MOLLE options, allowing personnel to maintain a lower profile without leaving a less-lethal option behind
•More options when intervention is necessary: Four individually deployed probes, compared to 10 for TASER 10, with a range from close contact to 45 feet (13.7 m), plus a green laser and built-in LED light for greater visibility



•Accountability built in: Activity logging for upload to Axon Evidence, with optional wireless sharing, alerts and weapon behavior data to help organizations align use with policy and authorized assignments

The breadth and impact of our product investments is increasingly visible in our financial results, with newer products and software becoming meaningful contributors alongside our core businesses.
Q3 2026 Summary Results
Total revenue increased 28% year over year to $909 million, led by Software & Services, which accelerated to 43% growth and reached $437 million. Connected Devices revenue increased 16% to $472 million, with growth moderating sequentially due to normal third-quarter seasonality and the timing of an approximately $30 million Federal Dedrone deployment we had expected to recognize during the quarter. As our counter-drone business scales, particularly with Federal and international customers, the size and hardware-heavy nature of these deployments can create greater quarter-to-quarter variability in revenue timing.

Total company gross margin of 56.7% decreased from 60.1% in the prior-year period, and Adjusted gross margin was 59.2%, compared to 62.7% in the prior-year period. The decrease in gross margin and Adjusted gross margin was primarily driven by an increased mix of counter-drone equipment, higher memory costs and scaling of new product categories.

Operating income increased to $42 million, compared to a loss from operations of $2 million in the prior-year period, reflecting higher revenue and operating leverage.
•Cost of goods sold of $394 million, or 43% of revenue, including $13 million of stock-based compensation expense
•SG&A expense of $280 million, or 31% of revenue, including $78 million of stock-based compensation expense
•R&D expense of $192 million, or 21% of revenue, including $65 million of stock-based compensation expense

Net income was $57 million (6.3% net income margin), or $0.68 per diluted share, compared to a net loss of $2 million (0.3% net loss margin) or ($0.03) per diluted share in the prior-year period.

Non-GAAP net income was $144 million (15.8% non-GAAP net income margin), or $1.72 per diluted share, compared to $103 million (14.5% non-GAAP net income margin) or $1.24 per diluted share in the prior-year period.

Adjusted EBITDA increased 33% year over year to $236 million, representing an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 25.9%. Adjusted EBITDA reflects continued operating leverage and a more favorable revenue mix led by accelerating Software & Services growth. This performance demonstrates our ability to deliver strong profitability even as we invest behind newer, hardware-intensive businesses with significant long-term growth potential.

Operating cash flow was $20 million, compared to $60 million in the prior-year period. This included approximately $100 million of cash used in the quarter related to our strategic inventory investment to support product availability and supply chain resilience, in addition to ongoing investment as we scale to support growth.

Free cash flow was an outflow of $19 million, compared to an inflow of $33 million in the prior-year period, primarily reflecting the impact of our inventory investment, which totaled approximately $100 million in the quarter.
As of September 30, 2026, Axon had $1.7 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments and $2.9 billion of outstanding convertible and senior notes, resulting in a net debt position of $1.2 billion, up $182 million sequentially.

Detailed definitions of our non-GAAP financial measures and caution regarding their use are included later in this letter.

Statistical Definitions
Annual recurring revenue: Annual recurring revenue is a performance indicator that management believes provides more visibility into the growth of our revenue generated by our highest margin, recurring services. Annual recurring revenue should be viewed independently of revenue and deferred revenue because it is an operating measure and is not intended to be combined with or to replace GAAP revenue or deferred revenue, as they can be impacted by contract start and end dates and renewal rates. Annual recurring revenue is not intended to be a replacement or forecast of revenue or deferred revenue. We calculate annual recurring revenue as monthly recurring license, integration, warranty and storage revenue, annualized.

Net revenue retention: Dollar-based net revenue retention is an important metric to measure our ability to retain and expand our relationships with existing customers. We calculate it as the software, camera and TASER warranty subscription and support revenue from a base set of agency customers from which we generated Axon Cloud subscription and warranty revenue in the last month of a quarter divided by the software and camera warranty subscription and support revenue from the year-ago month of that same customer base. This calculation includes high-margin warranty revenue but purposely excludes the lower-margin hardware subscription component of the customer contracts, as it is meant to be a SaaS metric that we use to monitor the health of the recurring revenue business we are building. This calculation also excludes the implied monthly revenue contribution of customers that were added since the year-ago quarter, and therefore excludes the benefit of new customer acquisition. The metric includes customers, if any, that terminated during the annual period, and therefore, this metric is inclusive of customer churn. This metric is downwardly adjusted to account for the effect of phased deployments — meaning that, for the year-ago period, we consider the total contractually obligated implied monthly revenue amount, rather than monthly revenue amounts that might have been in actuality smaller on a GAAP basis due to the customer not having yet fully deployed their Axon solution. For more information relative to our revenue recognition policies, please reference our filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

Future contracted bookings: This operational metric tracks our total unfulfilled contracted bookings, including remaining performance obligations, in addition to contracts with certain termination or other clauses that exclude them from remaining performance obligations. Total future contracted bookings for products and services represent total orders that the Company has received and not yet performed. Such cumulative gross bookings include amounts associated with third-party agent arrangements, where we may only recognize the net portion expected to be paid on behalf of our customers as revenue. The amounts associated with third-party agent arrangements not recognized will be eliminated from future contracted bookings upon fulfillment. This operational metric is subject to change based on future events, including terminations for convenience, the execution of optional periods or other contract modifications or cancellations. This operational metric may be unique to the Company, as it may be different from similarly titled operational metrics used by other companies. As such, the presentation of this operational metric may not enhance the comparability of the Company’s results to the results of other companies.

Bookings: This operational metric represents total product and service orders the Company received during the period, including customer contracts with certain termination or cancellation clauses, optional periods or other clauses, as well as customer orders associated with third-party agent arrangements. To facilitate comparison across end markets with varying contract durations, the Company also presents five-year bookings, which reduces the value of contracts with durations longer than five years to only include the value of the first five years in the contract. A contract with a duration of five years or shorter is included at its full contract value and not adjusted. The Company provides this metric as growth in newer end markets, including international and enterprise, increases the mix of contracts with shorter durations than those often signed in state and local public safety. Management believes five-year bookings provides a comparable view of underlying demand across end markets and periods.



Supplementary Non-GAAP Measures
To supplement the Company's financial results presented in accordance with GAAP, we present the non-GAAP financial measures of EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA margin, adjusted gross margin, non-GAAP net income, non-GAAP net income margin, non-GAAP diluted earnings per share, free cash flow and adjusted free cash flow. The Company's management uses these non-GAAP financial measures in evaluating the Company's performance in comparison to prior periods. We believe that both management and investors benefit from referring to these non-GAAP financial measures in assessing the Company's performance, and when planning and forecasting our future periods. A reconciliation of GAAP to the non-GAAP financial measures is presented below.
Beginning in the quarterly period ended March 31, 2026, we updated Adjusted EBITDA to exclude all components of other income (loss), net, including foreign currency exchange gains and losses, net and fees related to our Credit Agreement, as these items are not considered representative of our core operating results. Comparable prior periods were not recast because the impact was de minimis.
Beginning in the quarterly period ended March 31, 2026, we updated non-GAAP Net Income and non-GAAP Diluted Earnings per Share to exclude amortization of acquired intangible assets, as the portion of the purchase price assigned to the intangible assets and the corresponding amortization period can differ from one acquisition to another, and we do not consider this activity to be representative of our core ongoing operations. Comparable prior periods were recast, including the related income tax effects.
Beginning in the quarterly period ended June 30, 2026, we updated Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted Gross Margin to exclude jurisdiction-specific compensation-related taxes directly associated with Employee XSP vesting events, expanding our historical adjustment for payroll taxes to include such activities. Comparable prior periods were not recast because the impact was de minimis.
•EBITDA (most comparable GAAP measure: Net income) – Earnings before interest expense, investment interest income, income taxes, depreciation and amortization.
•Adjusted EBITDA (most comparable GAAP measure: Net income) – Earnings before interest expense; investment interest income; income taxes; depreciation; amortization; all components of other income (loss), net, which is primarily comprised of fair value adjustments and income or losses related to strategic investments and marketable securities, debt inducement expense associated with the early repurchase of a portion of our 2027 Notes, foreign currency exchange gains and losses, net, and fees incurred related to our Credit Agreement; noncash stock-based compensation expense; transaction and integration costs related to strategic investments and acquisitions, including the change in fair value of contingent consideration arrangements and certain compensation costs directly related to employee integration activities; non-recurring severance costs, including employee cash payments, equity, and related benefits; costs (or subsequent recoveries of prior costs) related to certain legal or regulatory matters we consider outside of our core operating activities; mark-to-market adjustments on our non-qualified deferred compensation liabilities; compensation taxes related to Employee XSP vesting; losses incurred as a result of the disposal, abandonment, and impairment of property, equipment, and intangible assets; and inventory step-up amortization related to acquisitions.
•Adjusted EBITDA margin (most comparable GAAP measure: Net income margin) – Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of net sales.
•Adjusted gross margin (most comparable GAAP measure: Gross margin) – Gross margin before noncash stock-based compensation expense; compensation taxes related to Employee XSP vesting; amortization of acquired intangible assets; non-recurring severance costs, including employee cash payments, equity, and related benefits; and inventory step-up amortization related to acquisitions.
•Non-GAAP net income (most comparable GAAP measure: Net income) – Net income excluding fair value adjustments and income or losses related to strategic investments and marketable securities; the costs of noncash stock-based compensation expense; amortization of acquired intangible assets; transaction and integration costs related to strategic investments and acquisitions, including the change in fair value of contingent consideration arrangements; compensation taxes related to Employee XSP vesting; costs (or subsequent recoveries of prior costs) related to certain legal or regulatory matters we consider outside of our core operating activities; non-recurring severance costs, including employee cash payments, equity, and related benefits; debt inducement expense associated with the early repurchase of a portion of our 2027 Notes; and inventory step-up amortization related to acquisitions. The Company tax-effects non-GAAP adjustments using the blended statutory federal and state tax rates for each period presented.



•Non-GAAP net income margin (most comparable GAAP measure: Net income margin) - Non-GAAP net income as a percentage of sales.
•Non-GAAP diluted earnings per share (most comparable GAAP measure: Earnings per share) – Measure of the Company's non-GAAP net income divided by the weighted average number of diluted common shares outstanding during the period presented.
•Free cash flow (most comparable GAAP measure: Cash flow from operating activities) – Cash flows provided by operating activities minus purchases of property and equipment.
•Adjusted free cash flow (most comparable GAAP measure: Cash flow from operating activities) – Free cash flow, excluding the net impact of investments in our new Scottsdale, Arizona campus and bond premium amortization.
◦We believe that free cash flow and adjusted free cash flow excluding the impact of bond premium amortization and net campus investment are non-GAAP measures that are useful to investors and management to evaluate the Company’s ability to generate cash. These non-GAAP measures can also be used to evaluate the Company’s ability to generate cash flow from operations and the impact that this cash flow has on the Company’s liquidity.
