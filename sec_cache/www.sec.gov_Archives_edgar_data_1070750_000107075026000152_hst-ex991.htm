HIGHLIGHTS:
•Comparable hotel Total RevPAR was $348.19 for the third quarter of 2026, an increase of 7.3% compared to the same period in 2025, driven by increases in room rates and continued growth in food and beverage spend. Growth was broad-based, with 11 of the Company's 24 domestic markets achieving double-digit increases in comparable hotel Total RevPAR. Comparable hotel Total RevPAR year-to-date in 2026 was $392.74, an increase of 5.8%.
•Comparable hotel RevPAR was $219.50, an increase of 8.8% compared to the same period in 2025, reflecting rate increases of 5.7% and an increase in occupancy of 2.1 points. The growth was driven by very strong transient demand throughout the quarter, boosted by the World Cup and an increase in business transient demand, in addition to an increase in group bookings, and despite the impacts from an active hurricane season in Hawaii during the quarter. Comparable hotel RevPAR year-to-date in 2026 was $237.21, an increase of 6.6%.
•GAAP net income was $71 million, a 56.4% decrease compared to the third quarter of 2025, reflecting a decline in gain on asset sales, partially offset by an improvement in GAAP operating profit margin of 140 basis points to 9.0%, as higher room rates offset wage expense increases. Year-to-date, GAAP net income was $813 million, a 27.2% increase compared to 2025, benefiting from gains on asset sales and GAAP operating profit margin of 15.8%, an improvement of 110 basis points compared to 2025.
•Comparable hotel EBITDA was $331 million, an increase of 11.4% compared to the third quarter of 2025, reflecting a comparable hotel EBITDA margin increase of 90 basis points to 25.2% due to improvements in operations, largely driven by average room rate increases, which offset increases in wage expenses over the same period in 2025. Year-to-date, comparable hotel EBITDA was $1,327 million, an increase of 8.2% compared to 2025, while comparable hotel EBITDA margin increased 70 basis points to 30.2%. Comparable hotel results for both periods exclude Alila Ventana Big Sur, which was removed from the comparable hotel set in the third quarter after the hotel was closed for one month during an evacuation mandate due to wildfires in the vicinity.
•Adjusted EBITDAre was $347 million, an increase of 8.8% compared to the third quarter of 2025. Results benefited from improved operations and comparable hotel EBITDA margins, which more than offset declines due to the 2025 and 2026 dispositions. Year-to-date Adjusted EBITDAre was $1,415 million, exceeding 2025 by 6.5%.
•Subsequent to quarter end, the Company acquired the leasehold interest in the 195-room 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge for a total purchase price of $202 million, which is net of the $4 million furniture, fixtures and equipment ("FF&E") reserve. The Company also acquired the fee simple interest in the 308-room Renaissance Dallas at Plano Legacy West Hotel for a total purchase price of $172 million. The 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge, a LEED Gold-certified luxury hotel that sits on Pier 1 in Brooklyn Bridge Park, overlooks the East River and the Manhattan skyline. The Renaissance anchors the Legacy West mixed-use development in Plano, Texas and has approximately 35,000 square feet of meeting space.
•Subsequent to quarter end, the Company amended and restated its $2.5 billion credit facility, extending the maturity of the $1.5 billion revolver to January 2031 and the maturities of the two $500 million term loans to January 2031 and January 2032, respectively, with extension options that, if exercised, would align all maturities under the facility at January 2032. Under the amended facility, the Company expanded the accordion feature to $1.25 billion and achieved total reductions of 12.5 basis points on revolver draws, 15 basis points on the term loans, and 5 basis points on the facility fee, reflecting both a reset of the pricing grid to current market levels and a new leverage-based pricing benefit, while maintaining pricing incentives linked to portfolio sustainability initiatives.
Sourav Ghosh, Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, added, "Subsequent to quarter end, we further strengthened our balance sheet by recasting our $2.5 billion credit facility, extending maturities through 2031 and 2032 and improving pricing. Following the recast, our debt has a weighted average maturity of 5.2 years and a weighted average interest rate of 4.8%. The enhancements to our credit facility provide further flexibility as we pursue our successful capital allocation strategy.”

2026 OUTLOOK
In the third quarter of 2026, the Company continued to see strong leisure and group demand, which drove an increase in rates, coupled with improving business transient volume. Comparable hotel RevPAR for October also grew approximately 6.5% over 2025. The updated 2026 guidance reflects the Company's strong third quarter results and improved expectations for the fourth quarter driven by strong leisure demand, favorable group booking pace, and improving business transient demand. Full year operating profit margins and comparable hotel EBITDA margins are expected to increase compared to 2025, as rate improvements offset increases in wage expense.
The guidance includes an estimated $3 million and $6 million of net income and Adjusted EBITDAre, respectively, which is expected from the recent acquisitions of 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge and Renaissance Dallas at Plano Legacy West Hotel. Due to the timing of the acquisitions, the results for these two hotels will be included in comparable hotel results starting in the fourth quarter. The guidance for net income and Adjusted EBITDAre also includes an estimated $18 million net contribution from sales expected to close at the condominium development adjacent to the Four Seasons Resort Orlando at Walt Disney World® Resort this year, with remaining sales expected to shift into 2027. Additionally, the final determination on insurance claims related to Hurricanes Helene and Milton is expected by the end of the year, but no additional amounts from what was received in first quarter are included in guidance.

ABOUT HOST HOTELS & RESORTS
Host Hotels & Resorts, Inc. is an S&P 500 company and is the largest lodging real estate investment trust and one of the largest owners of luxury and upper-upscale hotels. The Company currently owns 72 properties in the United States and five properties internationally totaling approximately 41,800 rooms. The Company also holds non-controlling interests in seven domestic joint ventures. Guided by a disciplined approach to capital allocation and aggressive asset management, the Company partners with premium brands such as Marriott®, Ritz-Carlton®, Westin®, W®, The Luxury Collection®, Hyatt®, Fairmont®, 1 Hotels®, Hilton®, Swissôtel®, ibis® and Novotel®, as well as independent brands. COMPARABLE HOTEL OPERATING STATISTICS AND RESULTS
To facilitate a year-to-year comparison of our operations, we present certain operating statistics (i.e., Total RevPAR, RevPAR, average daily rate and average occupancy) and operating results (revenues, expenses, hotel EBITDA and associated margins) for the periods included in our reports on a comparable hotel basis in order to enable our investors to better evaluate our operating performance. We define our comparable hotels as those that: (i) are owned or leased by us as of the reporting date and are not classified as held-for-sale; and (ii) have not sustained substantial property damage or business interruption, or undergone large-scale capital projects, in each case requiring closures lasting one month or longer (as further defined below), during the reporting periods being compared.
We make adjustments to include recent acquisitions to include results for periods prior to our ownership. For these hotels, since the year-over-year comparison includes periods prior to our ownership, the changes will not necessarily correspond to changes in our actual results. Additionally, operating results of hotels that we sell are excluded from the comparable hotel set once the transaction has closed or the hotel is classified as held-for-sale.
The hotel business is capital-intensive and renovations are a regular part of the business. Generally, hotels under renovation remain comparable hotels. A large-scale capital project would cause a hotel to be excluded from our comparable hotel set if it requires the entire property to be closed to hotel guests for one month or longer.
Similarly, hotels are excluded from our comparable hotel set from the date that they sustain substantial property damage or business interruption if it requires the property to be closed to hotel guests for one month or longer. In each case, these hotels are returned to the comparable hotel set when the operations of the hotel have been included in our consolidated results for one full calendar year after the hotel has reopened. Often, related to events that cause property damage and the closure of a hotel, we will collect business interruption insurance proceeds for the near-term loss of business. These proceeds are included in net gain on insurance settlements on our condensed consolidated statements of operations. Business interruption insurance gains covering lost revenues while the property was considered non-comparable also will be excluded from the comparable hotel results.
Of the 75 hotels that we owned as of September 30, 2026, 73 have been classified as comparable hotels. The operating results of the following properties that we owned as of September 30, 2026 are excluded from comparable hotel results for these periods:
•The Don CeSar (business disruption due to Hurricane Helene resulting in closure of the hotel beginning at the end of September 2024, reopened in March 2025);
•Alila Ventana Big Sur (business disruption due to local evacuation mandates and regional wildfire activity resulting in closure of the hotel beginning in August 2026, reopened in September 2026); and
•Operations related to the development and sale of condominium units on a development parcel adjacent to the Four Seasons Resort Orlando at Walt Disney World® Resort.

NAREIT FFO AND NAREIT FFO PER DILUTED SHARE
We present NAREIT FFO and NAREIT FFO per diluted share as non-GAAP measures of our performance in addition to our earnings per share (calculated in accordance with GAAP). We calculate NAREIT FFO per diluted share as our NAREIT FFO (defined as set forth below) for a given operating period, as adjusted for the effect of dilutive securities, divided by the number of fully diluted shares outstanding during such period, in accordance with NAREIT guidelines. As noted in NAREIT’s Funds From Operations White Paper – 2018 Restatement, NAREIT defines FFO as net income (calculated in accordance with GAAP) excluding depreciation and amortization related to certain real estate assets, gains and losses from the sale of certain real estate assets, gains and losses from change in control, impairment expense of certain real estate assets and investments and adjustments for consolidated partially owned entities and unconsolidated affiliates. Adjustments for consolidated partially owned entities and unconsolidated affiliates are calculated to reflect our pro rata share of the FFO of those entities on the same basis.
We believe that NAREIT FFO per diluted share is a useful supplemental measure of our operating performance and that the presentation of NAREIT FFO per diluted share, when combined with the primary GAAP presentation of diluted earnings per share, provides beneficial information to investors. By excluding the effect of real estate depreciation, amortization, impairment expense and gains and losses from sales of depreciable real estate, all of which are based on historical cost accounting and which may be of lesser significance in evaluating current performance, we believe that such measures can facilitate comparisons of operating performance between periods and with other REITs, even though NAREIT FFO per diluted share does not represent an amount that accrues directly to holders of our common stock. Historical cost accounting for real estate assets implicitly assumes that the value of real estate assets diminishes predictably over time. As noted by NAREIT in its Funds From Operations White Paper – 2018 Restatement, the primary purpose for including FFO as a supplemental measure of operating performance of a REIT is to address the artificial nature of historical cost depreciation and amortization of real estate and real estate-related assets mandated by GAAP. For these reasons, NAREIT adopted the FFO metric in order to promote a uniform industry-wide measure of REIT operating performance.
